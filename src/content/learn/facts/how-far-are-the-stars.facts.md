## The ancient argument
- Aristotle, On the Heavens II.14: "there would have to be passings and turnings of the fixed stars. Yet no such thing is observed." | 4th c. BCE | J. L. Stocks translation, MIT Classics, https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/heavens.2.ii.html | verified (primary, translation)
- Archimedes, Sand-Reckoner: Aristarchus's hypothesis that the Earth revolves about the Sun and the sphere of fixed stars is so great that the Earth's orbit bears to it the ratio a centre bears to a surface | 3rd c. BCE | T. L. Heath, The Works of Archimedes (Cambridge UP, 1897), pp. 221-222, https://archive.org/details/worksofarchimede00arch | verified (primary, translation)
- Copernicus (1543): no such phenomena appear in the fixed stars, which "proves their immense height" | 1543 | quoted in C. M. Graney, arXiv:1112.1988 (2011) | verified (secondary quoting primary; paraphrased in article)
- Tycho: positions to better than 1 arcmin; first-magnitude star 2 arcmin across; with parallax < 1 arcmin, Copernican stars ~7,000 au away (over 700 x Sun-Saturn) and hundreds of times the Sun's diameter; rejected Copernican appeals to God's power | n/a | Graney, arXiv:1112.1988; Fernie, JRASC 69, 153 (1975), https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1975JRASC..69..153F | verified (secondary)

- Galileo proposed (Dialogue, 1632) using close pairs of unequal brightness to find parallax | 1632 | Fernie 1975b, p. 227 | verified (secondary quoting primary)
